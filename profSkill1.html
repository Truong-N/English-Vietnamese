WHAT IS A MASTER LIST?
- Master list is a Tool
-- A Resume Master List is a comprehensive document containing all your professional experiences, skills, education, and achievements.
-- It serves as a single source to create tailored resumes and cover letters for specific job applications.
- To create one:
-- Gather all job details, training, volunteer work, projects, and skills.
-- Organize by category in reverse-chronological order.
-- (Start from this year and go back in time)
-- Update regularly throughout your career.
WHAT TO INCLUDE?
- A chronological list of relevant life experiences (paid work, volunteering, education and etc.).
- A brief analysis of each experience demonstrating your skills, knowledge, and traits.
- A summary of your skills, knowledge, and traits.
WHY CREATE A MASTER LIST?
- Although it may take time to compile
- It's one of the most useful tools for career development and job hunting.
- Focused resumes: Choose experiences that best highlight your skills.
- Easy recall: Keep a record of job titles, dates, and duties.
- Self-awareness: Identify work you enjoy and patterns in your career.
- Spot skill gaps: See which skills to develop for your dream job.
- Discover opportunities: A complete view of your skills may reveal roles you hadn't considered.
STEP 1: SELECT A YEAR
- Choose a timeframe (e.g., last 5 years).
- Note: Select a year-cycle (if you don't have much experience. E.G: 2020-2025)
- For extensive experience, list by individual year; for less, group into year cycles.
- Include everything: jobs, volunteering, school, training, projects.
- Tip: Don't filter just collect first
STEP 2: SELECT A CATEGORY
- Tier 1 (Essential): Paid Work, Volunteer Work, Formal Education
- Tier 2 (Supportive): Organizational Roles, Informal Learning, Personal Honors
- Tier 3 (Extra): Hobbies, Travel, Cultural Experiences, Family Responsibilities
- Note: Focus on Essential and Supportive Categories.
- Note: You can use the Master List Checklist Document and template to guide your creation of your Master List
STEP 3: DESCRIBE EACH EXPERIENCE
- Timeframe: Year or year cycle
- Category: Paid work, volunteering, etc.
- Experience #: 1, 2, 3... (Note: do at least 2 within each category).
- Role/Title: Optional
- What/When/Why (Describe an experience)
- Skills / Knowledge / Traits
- Tip: Tier 2 & 3 can skip timeline if desired.

STEP 5: BUILD STAR BULLETS
- Transform experiences into resume-ready statements using STAR (Acronym):
- S- Situation
- T- Task
- A- Action
- R- Result
Tip: STAR makes experiences ready for chronological or functional resumes
STAR SYSTEM EXPLAIN
- S-Situation
-- Set the context briefly. Example: Sales team was underperforming at XYZ Corp.
- T-Task
-- State your responsibility or goal. Example: Tasked with boosting quarterly sales.
- A-Action
-- Describe what you did. Example: 'Implemented CRM, trained team, reorganized territories.
- R-Result
-- Show measurable impact. Example: Increased sales by 35%, exceeding targets.
STAR EXAMPLE - WORK
- Original: Collected and analyzed student learning data.
- STAR Bullet: Analyzed kindergarten student progress data to identify learning gaps, improving teaching strategies and boosting overall literacy outcomes.
- Tip: Don't include the bracketed labels in your resume.
STAR EXAMPLE - VOLUNTEER
- Original: Assisted in organizing children’s activities.
- STAR Bullet: Organized storytelling and arts activities at Bhangra Jam event, creating a safe and engaging ENVIRONMENT FOR 50+ children.
STEP 6: REVIEW & SELECT
- Aim for 3 experiences per essential category (Work, Volunteer, Education).
- Review for clarity, impact, and skills.
- Select strongest entries for resumes, Linkedln, or interviews

WHAT IS A PORTFOLIO?
- A portfolio is a curated collection of documents and materials highlighting your skills, work experience, and achievements. 
- Portfolios can be physical or digital, depending on the type of work. 
For example, a painter may opt for a physical design portfolio, while a web designer might create a digital portfolio or portfolio website using platforms like Wix, Squarespace, or WordPress.
WHY IS PORTFOLIO IMPORTANT?
- A portfolio is crucial because it provides potential employers and clients with
a tangible showcase of your best work. 
For creative roles like graphic design, social media management, or web design, a portfolio offers a firsthand look at your capabilities, helping you stand out.
- Even if a job posting doesn't explicitly request a portfolio, having one prepared demonstrates initiative and professionalism.
- Portfolios are particularly valuable for roles such as:
- photographer
- graphic designer
- writer
- website designer
- social media manager
- software developer
- interior designer
- A well-designed portfolio, whether a one-page webpage or a multi-section digital portfolio, can also improve your SEO (search engine optimization) visibility, making it easier for recruiters to find you online via platforms like Linkedin.
WHAT TO INCLUDE IN A PORTFOLIO?
- To create a professional portfolio that resonates with potential employers, include:
- Career summary: A concise overview of your work experience and goals.
- Contact information: Your name, e-mail, phone number, and online profile.
- Certificates, licenses, degrees: Copies or mentions of your credentials.
- List of skills: Highlight relevant design skills or technical abilities.
- Work samples: High-quality examples of your best work, such as mockups or website templates.
- Professional achievements: A wards, recognitions, or successful projects.
- References or testimonials: Three to five endorsements from collaborators or clients
How TO MAKE A PORTFOLIO
- Write a Letter of Introduction. 
Start with a brief cover letter or “About Me” section outlining your background, values, and career goals. 
Include a short philosophy statement to show cultural fit.
Keep it concise so viewers quickly reach your work samples.
- Curate Your Best Work. 
Showcase your strongest, most relevant projects—such as design mockups, social media campaigns, or coding samples. 
Focus on quality over quantity. 
If new to the field, include school or personal projects (e.g., a Canva or Adobe portfolio).
Use sites like Wix or Squarespace for a professional presentation.
HOW TO MAKE A PORTFOLIO
- Include Professional References Feature 3-5 testimonials or reference letters from supervisors, clients, or collaborators. 
These endorsements demonstrate credibility, professionalism, and work ethic.
- Showcase Your Skills List key skills relevant to your target role, with short examples. Example: “Web Design — Built a WordPress portfolio optimized for SEO and user experience.' Specific examples make your skills tangible and aligned with job requirements
- Organize Your Portfolio Structure your content clearly with sections like Experience, Samples, and References. 
Use a table of contents for print versions or intuitive menus online. 
Place your resume, skills, and references before project samples for logical flow.
- Enhance Visual Appeal Design with consistency fonts, colours, and layout. 
For digital portfolios, use WordPress, Canvas, or Wix templates.
For print versions, use binders with tabs or colour coding.
Include high-quality visuals, screenshots, or mockups to elevate professionalism.
TIPS FOR CREATING A STRONG PORTFOLIO
- Update regularly. Refresh your portfolio every few months with new work samples or skills to keep it relevant.
- Make copies. For physical portfolios, create duplicates to leave with employers post-interview.
- Prepare for interviews. 
Review your portfolio to recall key achievements and skills, boosting your confidence.
- Customize for each role. Tailor your portfolio to highlight skills and samples relevant to the job; using job-specific portfolio templates.
WHY WRITE A COVER LETTER WHAT TO WRITE?
- One Goal:
- It will motivate your recruiter to read your resume!
What to write in a Cover Letter?
- Cover letters are meant to be persuasive, not just factual. 
A cover letter is a sales presentation in disguise.
- Ford R. Myers, Get the Job You Want, Even When No One's Hiring
WHY WRITE A COVER LETTER?
- Giving Recruiters What They Want
- Far from being pointless, the cover letter can be a game-changer. 
In the Resume Genius survey, 49% of hiring managers said a strong, persuasive cover letter could convince them to interview an otherwise weak candidate, and 33% said a weak cover letter could hurt an otherwise strong applicant.
- Cover Letters Still Matter - Even If They're Not Required
COMPONENTS OF A PERSUASIVE COVER LETTER
What to Include
- State the job clearly
-- Write the exact job title (and job number, if given).
-- Make sure it matches what's in the job posting.
Explain how you found the position
-- Say whether you saw it online, in an ad, or heard from someone.
-- If you mention a referral, only include people who can strongly recommend you.
- Share why you're interested
-- Explain what attracts you to the role or the organization.
-- You can mention personal reasons (like relocating) or positive things you've learned about the company
-Show why you're qualified
-- Highlight your key skills, education, and experiences that match the job.
-- Be specific and connect your background to what the job requires.
- Show you know the organization
-- Mention something meaningful you've learned about the company.
This shows you've done research and aren't just sending out the same cover letter everywhere.
COVER LETTER TEMPLATE
- Your first and last name.
- Your address: (Does not have To be specific, just city and province is sufficient)
- Your Phone, Email and Linkedin profile (hyperlink)
- Date:
- Employer's name
Employer's address
Re: (Job Title, reference number)
- Dear (contact person’s name):
- The first paragraph should explain why you are writing. 
Clearly say the position you want or the type of work you are looking for. 
Explain why you are interested in this company and this role. 
Also, explain why the employer would be interested in you. 
You can include how you learned about the job, such as from a job ad, a magazine, a friend, or a teacher.
- The middle paragraph(s), at most one to three in total, should show your skills, traits and experience using clear examples. 
Talk about work experience, volunteer work, school projects, activities, interests, education, or any other strengths that relate to the job. 
Focus on what you can do for the company, not just what you hope to gain. 
You can mention courses you took, your project, research, or fieldwork if they are relevant.
Make sure to show that you know about the company and understand the type of work it does.
- The last paragraph should thank the employer for considering your application. 
You can also say you hope for an interview. If the job is not advertised, you can write that you will call the company later to follow up.
- Sincerely,
(your signature)
Your name
COVER LETTER TIPS:
- It should be in “business casual.” (writing style).
-- A cover letter is a business document.
-- In fact, a cover letter should not be as formal as your résumé or an official job-offer letter, also known as a “letter of agreement”.
- You can use “I” but do so sparingly. 
Be service-centered rather than ego-centere.
- Cover Letter is NOT the same as a Résumé.
-- Your CL should let your employer see your skills, character and enthusiasm to work for this organization in this role.
