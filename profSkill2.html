WHAT IS A RESUME?
- A résumé is a summary of your academic background (formal education), paid and
unpaid work (volunteering) experience, achievements, and extra-curricular experiences.
- It is a strong statement of your skills, traits, professional experiences, presented in a way that demonstrates your 'fit' for the role you are applying to.
- The goal of your Résumé is to motivate your employer to invite you to an interview.
- A great résumé 'markets' your potential, based on your past experiences, education and
achievements
- You do not have to match all the required requirements of the position in order to get one
- However, you must communicate that your knowledge, skills and traits of character align closely with the goals of the role and company

RESUME TYPES
- Chronological Resume: Lists your work experience in reverse chronological order, highlighting your career progression and achievements. 
It's best for those who want to show a clear career path and have a steady work history.
- Functional Resume: Focuses on skills and abilities rather than work experience. 
It's ideal for individuals who have gaps in their work history or have recently
changed careers. 
This format emphasizes skills that match the job requirements.
- When to Use Each: Use a chronological resume if you have a solid work history and want to demonstrate your career progression. 
A functional resume is suitable if you have a strong skill set that aligns with the job you're applying for.

WHAT IS LMI?
- Labour Market Information (LMI) includes:
- Trends and conditions in industries or the local economy
- Occupational forecasts and hiring needs
- Organizations within industry sectors
- Required education, skills, and experience for jobs
- Salary information and workforce profile (age, education, gender, etc.)

THREE COMPONENTS OF LMI:
- Demand: Jobs available
- Supply: People available for jobs
- Forces affecting the market: e.g., laws and regulations
- Key questions to ask:
- Is there demand for my skills and qualifications?
- What skills, education, and experience do employers need?
- Will this field grow or decline?

HOW LMI HELPS EXPLORE CAREERS
- Research careers that match your interests, skills, and values
- Check trends and employment rate in specific fields
- Example: Is employment in education rising or falling?
-  Identify professions with strong future opportunities

How LMI HELPS FIND EMPLOYMENT
- Research job markets and growing industries
- Use employer directories to find contact information
- Examples:
- Which cities employ the most people in educational services?
- Are teachers hired more at junior, primary, or intermediate levels?

WHY NETWORK?
Most jobs are filled through networking rather than online job postings. 
Networking helps you explore careers, find opportunities, and improves your chances of success in your job search.

JOIN THE RIGHT COMMUNITY
- Why Communities Matter
- Communities multiply opportunities: instead of just adding contacts, you gain allies, mentors, collaborators, and access to resources.
- Collaboration allows specialisation and “gains from trade” — people can fécus on their strengths, share costs, and support each other.
- Even when members don’t share identical goals, trading knowledge or connections can benefit everyone.
- The culture of a community shapes your habits and values, so choosing the right one í crucial

CHOOSING THE RIGHT COMMUNITY
- Experiment with several groups and avoid relying only on one (to reduce echo-chamber effects).
- Communities can be professional, personal, or cause-based (e.g., biosecurity, animal welfare, social entrepreneurship, rationality).
- You can also create your own small community (reading groups, local meetups, online spaces).
- QUESTIONS:
1. What community, groups are you already part of?
2. Which community do you want to join or be part of?

It's important to understand how employers review these applications to increase your chances of getting an interview.

APPLICANT TRACKING SYSTEM (ATS) & YOUR RESUME
What is an ATS?
- Software that screens resumes for key skills and qualifications. 
- Almay be used to rank candidates; top matches are reviewed by a person.
Signs an ATS is Being Used
- Resume cut-and-paste requests or plain text submissions.
- Detailed online forms matching your resume.
- Employer mentions software or uses LinkedIn keyword searches

APPLICANT TRACKING SYSTEM (ATS) & YOUR RESUME
Tips for ATS-Friendly Resumes
- Use keywords from the job description.
- Include hard & soft skills in experience sections and summary.
- Use sans-serif fonts (e.g., Arial), avoid graphics, columns,
headers.
- Spell out acronyms; include dates for all experiences.
- PDF only if requested; otherwise use Word or text-friendly format.
Human Factor Matters
- Once through ATS, recruiters review formatting and clarity
- Don’t “trick” the ATS with random keywords.
- Include a strong cover letter highlighting skills and interest.

USING EMAIL FOR YOUR JOB SEARCH
Tip
Leave the recipient's address blank until you're ready to send. Avoid sending incomplete emails by accident.
Attachments
Send resumes in plain text, Word or PDF formats
Check the job posting or employer website for preferred format.
If unspecified. PDF is recommended to preserve formatting.

EMAIL ETIQUETTE
Cover Letter
Always include a brief cover letter explaining why you're applying and how your skills match the role.
Use professional salutation (e.g. "Dear Ms. Smith") and closings (e.g. "Sincerely")
Avoid emoticon, abbreviations, or casual language.
Contact information
Include full contact info in every email
Use a professional email address (e.g., your name, not nicknames).
Consider creating a signature file with your name, phone, and email.
Attachments & Body of Email
Attach resume (PDF preferred) and optionally paste cover letter in the email body.
Subject line: include position title.
Label attachments clearly with your full name.
Double-check your attached the connect resume version.
Inbox Management
Ensure you can receive replies: avoid full inboxes.
Check emails often including spam/junk folder.

EMAIL ETIQUETTE: APPLYING ONLINE VIA WEB FORMS
Key Points:
Web applications often include questions beyond your resume. e.g. GPA career goals, relevant skills, and extracurriculars.
Answer every question, don't leave boxes blank.
Be prepared to showcase skills and connect your education/experience to the role.
Use complete sentence, correct spelling, and proper grammar.
Tip: Review questions in advance and draft responses before submitting.

COLD CALLING & EMAILING
- Cold calling, emailing or messaging is the practice of contacting potential employers directly by phone, by email, or by direct messages sent within platforms such as Linkedln, without any prior contact, to find out about potential opportunities.
- This is best accomplished by identifying and understanding your skills, and how they are relevant to  the employers you approach and the jobs they may have available.

PURPOSE OF COLD CALLING AND EMAILING
- Most jobs are not advertised - these make up the hidden job market.
- Cold calling emailing and messaging help you reach opportunities directly.
- Combine these strategies with networking to leverage personal contacts and referrals.
- Effective for connecting with people who can hire you or introduce you to others.

STEPS TO EFFECTIVE COLD CALLING & EMAILING STRATEGY:
- Getting started
- Prepare your resume: highlight skills, qualities, and relevant experiences.
- Make a target list:  research companies and prepare questions to ask.
Organize your outreach: track who you contacted when what was discussed and follow-up.
-Reflect and improve:
review notes after each call/email to refine your approach.

EFFECTIVE COLD CALLING & EMAILING STRATEGIES
- Finding contact
Where to  look:
Directories: industry career, or location based lists
Linkedln: find companies and employees
Personal Network: professors, family, friends, neighbours, classmates.
Target the right person
Identify who handles hiring in your area of interest
Research: company website, linkedln, directories, informational interviews
Use research to generate questions to calls or emails
Tips for Reception calls
Ask only for the name and title or the hiring contact
Say you are researching the company if asked
Confirm spelling and pronunciation of names.

EFFECTIVE COLD CALLING & EMAILING STRATEGIES
Prepare Your Marketing Presentation (Elevator Pitch)
(Purpose):
- Structure your call and ensure you cover key points.
- Not a script to read verbatim—use it as a guide.
Key Elements (20 seconds):
- Introduction: State your full name and address the contact properly (Mr./Ms. or full name).
- Timing Question: Ask if it’s a good time to talk or schedule a better time. Mention you'll be brief.
- Brief Background: Share relevant experience and express interest in
the company.
- Request Advice/Information: Ask about potential positions or guidance.
Tip: Have your resume ready and be prepared to expand if asked.

EFFECTIVE COLD CALLING & EMAILING STRATEGIES
Making and Managing the call
Direct Connection:
Confirm it's a good time before starting your pitch
Take notes during the car
Thank the employer and ask about follow-up after finishing a dialogue.
Review notes afterward and plan next steps
Voicemail
State: our name, date/time and a brief message
Leave a contact number or indicate when you'll call back
Be prepared to answer their return call with notes and resume ready.
Ensure your voicemail is set up to receive replies.

EFFECTIVE COLD CALLING & EMAILING STRATEGIES
Steps for Effective Outreach
Identity the Right Contact: Use company websites, directories, or Linkedin.
State Your Purpose: Introduce yourself, explain how you got their contact, and why you're interested.
Personalize: Mention their work or achievements to show genuine interest.
Keep It Short: Brief and clear messages are more likely to be read.
(200 word in Linkedin)
Show Appreciation: Thank them for their time.
Follow-Up: Send one short follow-up after a week if no response; avoid excessive follow-ups.

FORM -EFFECTIVE SMALL TALK
The FORM Acronym
- F-Family/Friends
-- Ask about family, upbringing, or friends.
-- Example: 'Where are you from?' or 'How did you get into this line of work?'
- O-Occupation
-- Discuss their job or business to find common ground.
-- Example: 'What's your favorite part of your job?'
- R - Recreation
-- Inquire about hobbies, interests, or vacations.
-- Example: 'Have you been watching any good shows lately?'
- M-Motivation/Message
-- Connect on deeper levels: motivation, dreams or goals.
-- Example: 'What keeps you motivated in your field?'

FORM -EFFECTIVE SMALL TALK
Why use it?
- Helps start and maintain conversations
- Builds rapport quickly in networking or interviews
Activities (5 minutes each):
- Partner up and take turns interviewing each other using FORM questions
- Third person observes and notes effective listening and follow-up questions
- Rotate roles so everyone practices

FORM -EFFECTIVE SMALL TALK
Example Questions:
- Family: Where did you grow up?
- Occupation: What do you like most about your work or studies?
- Recreation: What do you enjoy doing outside of school/work?
- Motivation: What's something you're working toward this year?
- Goal: Practice genuine curiosity and connection through conversation

NON-VIOLENT COMMUNICATION
Practice Nonviolent Communication (NVC)
(Marshall Rosenberg)
- Observation - Describe what you see/hear without judgment
- Feeling - Express how you feel about what's happening
- Need - Identify what you need or value in the situation
- Request - Ask clearly and respectfully for what you want

NON-VIOLENT COMMUNICATION
Show Respect in Communication:
- Seek to understand before being understood
- Avoid bias, judgment, or interrupting
- Listen with openness and empathy
