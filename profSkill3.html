      INTERVIEW
WHAT IS AN INTERVIEW?
- A professional interview is a marketing situation,
where you can sell yourself To an organization.
- No matter what position the interview is for, preparation is key.
- An interview should be a business meeting and not an interrogation.
- This means that you have the opportunity to ask questions to decide whether the job is right for you.
- Interviews are a process with before, during, and after components.
- This presentation will focus on preparing before the interview.

TYPES OF INTERVIEWS
- Interviews can take many forms. Common types include:
- Behavioral - Questions about how you acted in past situations.
- Panel - More than one interviewer
- One-on-one - Single interviewer.
- Case - Problem-solving scenarios.
- Group - More than one candidate with one or more interviewers.
- Telephone or video - Skype, Zoom, MS Teams, etc.
- Recorded one-way video - You record answers for the employer to review.
- Tip: Ask ahead about the interview format and who will be interviewing you. Write down names and positions.

PREPARING FOR THE INTERVIEW
1. Know yourself and how you fit with the position.
- (You may use the Position Requirements Analysis Tool (PDF available)
- What role-specific skills (e.g. lab skills, editing skills, teaching skills) experience and education do you have that would fit the position? 
Think of skills that you have that match what the employer's looking for.
Think of concrete examples (experiences) when you demonstrated them.

PREPARING FOR THE INTERVIEW
- What skills that can be used in many jobs have you built (e.g., communication, time management, leadership)?
- These may come from:
- Paid work, Volunteering, School of academic projects, Clubs or other activities, Accomplishments


PREPARING FOR THE INTERVIEW
2. Know the Organization and Industry
- Research the company, using their website.
Consider speaking directly to company's representatives.
- Use Company Research Tool (PDF available) to help you conduct your research

PREPARING FOR THE INTERVIEW
3. Prepare answers to typical questions
- Types of Interview Questions:
- Open-ended - Broad questions (e.g. Tell me about yourself.)
- Behavioral - Ask about how you handled situations in the past.
- Situational/Hypothetical - Ask what you would do in a future situation.
- EDI (Equity, Diversity & Inclusion) - Questions about working with people from different backgrounds.

PREPARING FOR THE INTERVIEW
Use examples to show your skills.
For behavioural questions, use the STAR method:
- S-Situation: What was the context?
- T-Task: What needed to be done?
- A-Action: What steps did you take?
- R-Result: What was the outcome?

RESEARCH QUESTIONS TO ANSWER BEFORE AN INTERVIEW
- What does the organization do?
-- Nature of its business or services.
- What have they achieved?
-- History and major accomplishments.
- Where are they going?
-- Plans for the next year, goals, strategy, growth.
- Who are their competitors?
- How do they define success?

RESEARCH QUESTIONS TO ANSWER BEFORE AN INTERVIEW
- What are their values?
- What is their mission statement?
- Are there any changes happening in the organization?
- What trends might affect them?
-- Industry, labor market, or technology changes.

HOW TO RESPOND TO THEM
- 4. Prepare Questions for Your Interview:
- Ask Questions at the End of Your Interview
- Always come prepared with 3-5 questions
- At the end of an interview, you will often be asked: Do you have any questions for us?
- Asking questions shows:
-- You are prepared
-- You are interested in the job/company
-- You can learn more beyond the job description
 
HOW TO RESPOND TO THEM
- Types of Questions to Ask
-- Interest, Fit, Smile/Positive, Re-emphasize Points
- What not to Ask about:
- Salary, benefits, anything easily found in job description
- Reframe positively: Thank you, I don't have any further questions, I am ready to hear about further steps.

TYPES OF QUESTIONS TO ASK
1. Show interest
- Use your research to ask about company news or projects.
2. Decide fit
- Ask about key tasks, priorities, skills to develop, or compay culture.
- Examples:
-- What are the key priorities for this role?
-- What characteristics make someone successful here?

TYPES OF QUESTIONS TO ASK
3. Smile Questions
- Ask about positive aspects of the team or work.
- Examples:
-- What do you enjoy most about your work?
-- What are you most proud of in the last six months?
4. Re-emphasize Points
- Clarify or add information you missed earlier in the interview. (Only for important points).
TIP: 
5. Practice to become better!

PREPARING FOR AN INTERVIEW
- Identify your key strengths that match the job and organization.
- Anticipate questions and practice your answers.
- Prepare questions to ask the interviewer.

INTERVIEW STRUCTURE:
- Typical Interview Questions
-- Interviews often follow a common structure, no matter the format.
1. Icebreaker/Introduction
--Interviewers want you to feel comfortable.
-- Expect 'small talk questions:
--- Hobbies, weather, news, parking, etc.
-- Don't be discouraged if the interviewer is quiet or serious.

QUESTIONS ABOUT YOU
- General questions about skills, experience, and goals:
-- What are your strengths and weaknesses?
-- What type of environment do you work best in?
-- What are your career goals?
- Sometimes you will be asked to relate skills to specific tasks:
-- How long would it take to edit 2000 words?
-- How would you analyze current market conditions?
- Tip: Pause, think about the skill they want, and answer with a STAR example (Situation, Task, Action, Result).

BEHAVIOURAL QUESTIONS
- focus on past behavior as a predictor of future performance.
- Use STAR to structure answers:
-- Tell me about a time you handled an upset customer or co-worker.
-- Describe a stressful situation you managed.
-- Give an example of using good judgment to solve a problem.
-- tell me about a time you learned something new quickly.

CASE INTERVIEWS
- Common in Consulting and Finance.
- Problem-solving on the spot to test:
-- Logical thinking, business knowledge, creativity, communication.
- Types of case questions:
-- Brain Teasers: e.g., find the heavier ball among 8 using a scale twice.
-- Market Sizing: e.g., estimate how many golf balls were lost in England.
-- Projects: e.g., advise a supermarket chain opening bank branches.

UNSTRUCTURED INTERVIEWS
- Informal, few questions, sometimes closed-ended:
-- Example: Do you have experience preparing spreadsheets?
- Tip: Link answers to your STAR examples wherever possible.

CLOSURE & QUESTIONS YOU ASK
- Prepare 3-5 thoughtful questions in advance.
- Shows interest and knowledge of the company.
- Examples:
-- What are this year's goals for this role/department?
-- How does the company support learning and professional development? (If you feel competent and that you fit the role and company)
-- How do you foster and inclusive and supportive workplace?

DURING AN INTERVIEW
- Greet warmly with eye contact and a smile.
- Use positive body language: sit upright, nod, lean slightly forward.
- Be aware of interviewer signals: finish points when they're ready to move on.
- Be honest, ask for clarification, or request a moment to think if needed.
- For video interviews, maintain eye contact with the camera, not a script.

DURING AN INTERVIEW
Ask for Time if Needed
- Politely say: Could I have a moment to consider that?
Maintain Professionalism
- Use appropriate language throughout the interview.

DURING AN INTERVIEW
Express Gratitude and Follow Up
- Thank the interviewer(s) for the opportunity.
- Ask politely for contact information or connect via Linkedln.
- Send a personalized thank-you email within 24 hours.
- Reiterate interest and mention something specific from the interview.

DURING AN INTERVIEW
General Questions
- Why are you interested in this role?
- What do you know about our company?
- Greatest strengths/weaknesses?
- Short- and long-term goals?
Self-Introduction (PAWS Formula)
P: Personal - who you are and why you're interested
A- Academic - education or training related to the role
W- Work - relevant work/ volunteer experience
S- Skill - your strengths for the role

DURING AN INTERVIEW
Behavioural Questions (STARR Formula)
- S-Situation - describe the scenario
- T-Task - explain the challenge
- A:Action - What you did
- R-Result - what happened
- R-Relevance - relate skills to the role
- Example: Describe a time you worked in a team.
Situational Questions: 
- Hypothetical scenarios: What would you do if ...?
- Use STARR to structure responses and connect skills to the role.
- You can reference real-life experiences where appropriate.

DRESS CODE
First Impressions Matter
- First impressions count in interviews, networking events, job fairs and information interviews.
- A key part of making a strong impression is how you dress.
Tip for Professional Appearance
- Choose clean, neat, and professional clothing.
- Dress in a way that fits the workplace culture.
- Avoid clothing that is too casual or distracting.
- Pay attention to grooming and personal hygiene.

DRESS CODE
Basic Expectations
- Every interview or event is different.
- Research what's appropriate before attending,
- Aim to dress slightly above the basic expectation to make a strong impression.
Interview
- Dress for the most important day on the job (e.g., meeting the CEO or a client).
- If unsure:
-- Check the company's website.
-- Recall how staff dressed on past visits.
-- Copy the style of the best-dressed employees

OTHER PROFESSIONAL TIPS
Budget Tips for Professional Attire
- Employers understand students often have a limited budget.
- 1-2 well-chosen outfits are enough for interviews and events.
- Mix and match clothing items to create multiple looks
- Consider vintage or second-hand stores for affordable options.

EMOTIONAL REGULATION (TONE) & NON-VERBAL COMMUNICATION (BODY LANGUAGE)
Tips
- Pause before answering difficult questions.
- Reflect on past experiences and what you learned.
- Be genuine and tie experiences to personal growth.
Key: Emotional intelligence - managing yourself + relating well to others.

VERBAL & NON-VERBAL COMMUNICATION
Eye Contact
- Direct, natural eye contact shows confidence and sincerity.
- Avoid staring or looking away too often.
Body Language
- Sit upright, nod, lean slightly forward.
- Avoid folded arms or cowering: handshake should be firm.
